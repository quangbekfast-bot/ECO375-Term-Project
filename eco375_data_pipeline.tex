\section{Step 0: cleaning and matching news to prices}

Script \var{News Data Set/build\_news\_price\_dataset.py}; output \var{news\_price\_matched.csv} (1.3 GB), one row per (ticker, article).
\begin{itemize}
  \item Rows without a ticker dropped (general Reuters / Bloomberg / lenta.ru news; issue \#1).
  \item Dates outside 2009 -- last price date dropped (placeholder dates such as 1969-12-31; issue \#6).
  \item Each (ticker, article URL) kept once; the Nasdaq copy wins over duplicates (issues \#2, \#11).
  \item Tickers tagged by the 30 Nov -- 16 Dec 2023 scraping batch dropped (issue \#5).
  \item \textbf{Matching rule:} the publication date is matched to the first trading day on or after it, at most 5 days later. Weekend / holiday news lands on the next session. Articles with no price within 5 days are dropped (issue \#9).
\end{itemize}

%==========================================================
\section{Headline filter (price-report removal)}

Script \var{headline\_classifier.py}. Purpose: remove news that \emph{reports} the stock's price move, because counting it would let returns cause attention (reverse causality). Each headline gets one of three labels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{price\_report}: a roundup or technical item (52-week highs, ``stocks moving'', RSI, moving averages, gainers/losers), or a headline naming a price move with no stated cause. \textbf{Dropped.}
  \item \textbf{event\_plus\_price}: names a move \emph{and} a cause (earnings, FDA approval, acquisition, upgrade\ldots). \textbf{Kept}: the event is real news.
  \item \textbf{no\_price}: everything else. \textbf{Kept.}
\end{itemize}
Rules are regular expressions, checked by hand on samples. Effect on the sample: 16.1\% of articles removed (weekly panel, Mar 2018 -- Feb 2020: 1,139,097 raw vs.\ 955,153 filtered). The filter works on headlines only, not article text.

\section{Step 5b: daily news panel}\label{sec:5b}

Script \var{05b\_news\_daily\_panel\_matched.py}; input \var{news\_price\_matched.csv}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Date = the matched \emph{trading} day, so the news panel lines up with the price files.
  \item Window: trading days 1 Feb 2017 -- 28 Feb 2020 (Feb 2017 -- Feb 2018 is pre-sample history; extended from Dec 2017 on 17 Nov 2026 for the 52-week weekly windows).
  \item Two outputs, \var{news\_daily\_raw} and \var{news\_daily\_filtered}: 1,586,716 vs.\ 1,344,847 articles (776,485 vs.\ 696,384 ticker-days with news). Of the matched articles, 241,869 are price reports (15.2\%) and 42,432 event-plus-price.
  \item Sparse: a row exists only for ticker-days with at least one article. Zeros are filled in step 7.
\end{itemize}

\section{Step 7: central panels (news + prices)}

Script \var{07\_central\_panel.py} (run in batches \var{0 3}, \var{1 3}, \var{2 3}, then \var{merge daily}, \var{merge blocks}). Six outputs: \var{panel\_\{daily,weekly,monthly\}\_\{raw,filtered\}\_2018-03\_2020-02.csv}. Raw and filtered differ only in \var{n\_articles}; price columns are identical.

\subsection{Rows and coverage}
\begin{itemize}
  \item One row per ticker per trading day (or block) on which the ticker has a price, for every ticker with at least one article from Dec 2017 on: 5,521 tickers (the same set in all three panels; the longer weekly history adds no tickers).
  \item \var{covered} = 1 if the day lies between the ticker's first and last article in the whole news history. Outside that window the scraper did not collect news, so ``no article'' is not a real zero (issue \#4).
  \item \var{n\_articles}: count of articles; \textbf{0} on no-news days inside coverage, \textbf{blank} outside it (about 4\% of sample rows). For blocks, blank unless every trading day in the block is covered.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Time blocks}\label{sec:blocks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fixed, non-overlapping runs of consecutive calendar days, starting Saturday 3 Mar 2018. \textbf{Week} = 7 days (Saturday--Friday, so weekend news counts toward the next trading week); \textbf{month} = 30 days.
  \item Complete blocks only: 104 weeks (3 Mar 2018 -- 28 Feb 2020) and 24 months (3 Mar 2018 -- 20 Feb 2020).
  \item \textbf{Pre-sample} (\var{pre\_sample} = 1): periods before the first cross-section, kept so lagged and rolling-window variables exist from period 1. \textbf{Weekly: 52 weeks, blocks $-51..0$ (4 Mar 2017 -- 2 Mar 2018)}, set by the longest window in the model (52-week volatility); the 12-week size and momentum windows and the 3-week attention average fit inside it. Daily: 3 trading days (26--28 Feb 2018). Monthly: blocks $-2..0$ (3 Dec 2017 -- 2 Mar 2018). Analysis sample: \var{pre\_sample} = 0.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Gap rule for daily returns}\label{sec:gap}
A daily return is left blank when the previous price row is more than 180 calendar days earlier. Some price files carry stale rows from long before an IPO or from an earlier user of the ticker (gaps of 263--956 days in the data), which produced fake first-week returns (e.g.\ VEL $+$108,233\%, NET, TXG, VIR at their 2019 IPOs). Thinly traded funds can go weeks between trades (gaps up to about 80 days); those returns are real and are kept. Effect: 17 block returns changed in each of the weekly and monthly sample periods, 14 of which had been flagged \var{bad\_px}; every other price and count is identical to the previous build.

\subsection{Step 8 and storage}
\var{08\_sort\_by\_time.py} re-sorts each panel by period, then ticker, so each period is one contiguous cross-section (values read and written as text, nothing reformatted). The daily (276 MB) and weekly (135 MB) panels are stored on GitHub gzipped (85 MB and 45 MB); pandas reads the \var{.gz} directly; for Stata, unzip first (e.g.\ 7-Zip).

\section{Step 9: SEC controls (book equity, market equity, earnings)}\label{sec:step9}

Script \var{09\_sec\_controls.py}, stages: \var{index}; \var{names} (4 batches); \var{match}; \var{facts} (8 batches, plus \var{new}); \var{subs} (8 batches, plus \var{new}); \var{build}. Outputs \var{sec\_controls\_\{weekly,monthly\}\_2018-03\_2020-02.csv}, one row per ticker-block of the panels. Merge in Stata on \var{ticker} + \var{block}.

\subsection{Point-in-time rules}
Only information public by the block's last day is used.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \var{book\_equity}: \var{StockholdersEquity} (fallback: the tag including non-controlling interest) from the latest 10-Q/10-K filed on or before \var{block\_end}.
  \item \var{shares\_out}: cover-page share count (share classes summed), fallback balance-sheet count, then weighted-average basic shares; latest filed on or before \var{block\_end}.
  \item \var{market\_equity} = \var{shares\_out} $\times$ \var{split\_factor} $\times$ \var{close\_end}. Book-to-market = \var{book\_equity} / \var{market\_equity}.
  \item \var{earn\_8k} = 1 if an earnings-release 8-K (Item 2.02) was filed in the block; \var{filing\_10qk} = 1 if a 10-Q/10-K was filed in the block. Both are \textbf{blank} for weekly blocks before Nov 2017: filing histories were read from Nov 2017 on, so earlier weeks are unknown, not zero. They are only needed from period 1.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Split correction}
FNSPID prices are adjusted for splits up to about July 2020; SEC share counts are as reported at the time. So a share count filed before a split (and before July 2020) is multiplied by that split's ratio. Splits are detected when a later filing restates the same period's weighted-average share count at a split ratio, and dated at the first cover page whose share count jumps by that ratio (\var{split\_approx} = 1 where no jump was found). 112 split events; examples check out (ACGL 3:1 in 2018, AEYE 1:25 reverse).

\subsection{Ticker matching}
\begin{itemize}
  \item First pass: the SEC's current ticker list (2,950 tickers kept). A CIK whose first filing is after Feb 2020 is rejected as a later re-use of the ticker (31 rejected).
  \item Second pass: company names in headlines (``Name (TICKER)'') matched to SEC company names in use during 2017--2020 (824 tickers added).
  \item Override: where headlines consistently name a different company than the current SEC list, the headline company wins (21 tickers, all checked by hand: e.g.\ COHR = Coherent Inc, not II-VI; B = Barnes Group, not Barrick; GOLD = Barrick; AMTD = TD Ameritrade).
  \item Total matched: 3,795 of 5,521 tickers. Unmatched are mostly ETFs, funds and foreign listings.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cleaning fixes applied}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A form and its amendment filed the same day are not added together (had doubled CCK's share count).
  \item A filing that only re-reports an older period (e.g.\ an 8-K recasting past years) cannot replace a newer number (CDAY; 251 share and 220 book-equity values dropped).
  \item Fallback share counts more than 1.5$\times$ away from the cover-page count dropped (CTXS had tagged shares issued; 168 dropped).
  \item Values more than 200$\times$ the firm's median dropped as unit errors (108 share counts, 194 book-equity values).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Coverage and flags}
\begin{itemize}
  \item 68.5\% of ticker-blocks matched; book equity present in 54\%, market equity in 58\% of all ticker-blocks. The weekly file now has 823,449 rows (one per weekly panel row, including the 52 pre-sample weeks); its rows for the earlier blocks are unchanged.
  \item \var{foreign\_filer} = 1 (files 20-F/40-F; share count may be ordinary shares, not ADRs, e.g.\ BABA): 11.7\% of matched rows. \var{cik\_shared} = 1 (several tickers on one filer, mostly ETF trusts): 3.2\%. Market equity is unreliable for both; drop them.
  \item After dropping both flags: 3,209 firms; book equity present 84.8\%, market equity 85.3\%. B/M median 0.43 (1st--99th percentile $-0.62$ to 3.69); about 5\% negative book equity.
  \item Earnings: 6.0\% of weeks and 24.3\% of months have \var{earn\_8k} = 1. About a quarter of matched firms never file Item 2.02 8-Ks; for them use \var{filing\_10qk} (rougher: a 10-Q often comes after the announcement).
  \item Remaining market-equity jumps not explained by price: about 280 of 84,000 monthly rows, mostly real mergers (FIS--Worldpay, FISV--First Data, L3--Harris, NEM--Goldcorp) plus a few reverse splits the SEC data does not reveal (NVAX, PHIO, COOP).
\end{itemize}

\section{Step 10: headline sentiment (FinBERT)}\label{sec:sentiment}

\textbf{Goal.} Label each headline in the \emph{filtered} weekly panel positive, negative or neutral, then count per ticker-week: \var{agg\_positive}, \var{agg\_negative}, \var{agg\_neutral}. Check: the three sum to \var{n\_articles} in every row (with an \var{agg\_unscored} column if any headline has no score, so a gap is visible rather than silently breaking the sum). About 1.34 million filtered headlines, Mar 2017 -- Feb 2020.

\textbf{Status.} FNSPID does not provide its GPT scores, and rerunning its GPT-3.5 script needs a paid API key; a paid hosted FinBERT service caps requests at 300--2,000 per day (8 months to 4.5 years for our headlines). So we run the open-source FinBERT model ourselves, free, in Google Colab. \textbf{Done 17 Nov 2026} (results and audit in Section~\ref{sec:sentaudit}).

\textbf{Workflow.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \var{10a\_sentiment\_input.py}: the 1,344,847 filtered ticker-article rows (Mar 2017 -- Feb 2020) use only \textbf{685,259 distinct headlines} (487,152 first seen in the sample period), because one headline is often tagged to several tickers. Each distinct headline is scored once. Outputs \var{finbert\_input\_headlines.csv.gz} (21 MB) and \var{article\_headline\_map.csv.gz} (which rows use which headline).
  \item \var{finbert\_colab.ipynb} (Colab, free T4 GPU): \var{ProsusAI/finbert}, three labels directly, probabilities kept. Headlines are short (median $\sim$15 tokens, longest $\sim$91 vs.\ the model's 512 limit), so nothing is cut off. Progress is saved to Google Drive every 50,000 headlines and a rerun resumes. Tested here on a stand-in model: resume after an interruption works, and batched scores match one-at-a-time scores exactly.
  \item \var{10b\_merge\_sentiment.py}: copies each headline's label to every row that uses it, counts per ticker-week, adds the four columns to the filtered weekly panel and stops if they do not sum to \var{n\_articles}. Tested with placeholder labels: sums hold in all 781,667 covered rows; existing columns unchanged.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Method options}, in the order I would use them:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.2cm} L L@{}}
\toprule
Method & Strengths & Weaknesses\\
\midrule
1. FNSPID pre-scored GPT sentiment (if found) & Same source as our news; scores 1--5 map to 3 buckets (1--2 negative, 3 neutral, 4--5 positive); no compute & Coverage may not include all our tickers / dates; scored on article summaries, not headlines; GPT output not exactly reproducible\\
2. FinBERT (Araci, 2019; Huang, Wang \& Yang, 2023) & Finance-trained language model; three classes natively (positive / negative / neutral), so no bucket merging; free and reproducible; handles negation and context better than word lists & Needs the model download from HuggingFace: run it on your own machine or Google Colab, not here. 1.3M headlines is a few hours on a laptop CPU, much less on a Colab GPU\\
3. Loughran--McDonald dictionary (Loughran \& McDonald, 2011) & The standard finance word lists; transparent and fully reproducible; runs here in minutes (\var{pysentiment2} is installable); rule: more positive than negative words $\Rightarrow$ positive, and vice versa & Headlines are short, so many score neutral; no negation (``not profitable''); misses context\\
4. GPT-style LLM on headlines (Lopez-Lira \& Tang, 2023, \emph{Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?}) & Closest published precedent for headline-level LLM scores and return prediction & Paid API, 1.3M calls; results depend on the model version\\
5. VADER (Hutto \& Gilbert, 2014) & Fast, handles negation and intensity & Built for social media, not finance wording\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\textbf{Recommendation.} FinBERT is the main measure; Loughran--McDonald can serve as a robustness check: if both give the same sign for the attention--return result, the result does not hinge on one scoring method.

\subsection{Audit design}
An independent agent labeled 500 headlines \emph{blind} (it never saw FinBERT's labels, and confirmed it did not open the file holding them), reading each one and judging the tone for the company's shareholders, the same standard FinBERT is trained on. Sample: 120 random headlines from each FinBERT label, 90 low-confidence headlines (top probability $<$ 0.55) and the 50 headlines tagged to the most tickers. Each headline also got a flag: \emph{ok}, \emph{ambiguous}, \emph{multi-firm} (roundup), \emph{not a headline} (several items run together, truncated text) or \emph{price report} (missed by our filter). File: \var{Panel Build/data/sentiment\_audit\_sample.csv}.

The audit is a second reader, not ground truth: where the two disagree, either can be wrong.

\textbf{Reading.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Positive labels are reliable} (85\% agreement). \textbf{Negative labels are the weakest} (57\%): FinBERT reacts to negative \emph{words} even when the headline is mixed (``Beats on Q3 Earnings, Sales Fall Shy'') or bullish overall (``Dow Suffers Record Slump: 5 Value Picks to Buy on the Dip'').
  \item \textbf{Neutral misses opinion pieces}: ``Should You Buy?'', ``Worth Holding on to'', ``We Are Staying Away From Halliburton'' read as neutral to FinBERT, as clear calls to the audit.
  \item Disagreement concentrates where headlines are genuinely hard (51\% agreement on flagged headlines vs.\ 72\% on clear-cut ones), so part of it is ambiguity, not model error.
\end{itemize}

\section{Known data problems still open}

From \var{DATASET\_ISSUES.md}, the ones that affect our sample:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\#3 No time of day:} 99.1\% of timestamps are 00:00:00, so we cannot tell pre-market from after-close news. This is why attention and returns are measured over whole blocks, and why the prediction form (attention at $t$, return at $t+1$) is the cleaner timing.
  \item \textbf{\#4 Coverage starts and stops:} handled by \var{covered} and blank \var{n\_articles} outside a ticker's coverage window.
  \item \textbf{\#7 Roundup headlines} that only mention the company: partly caught by the price-report filter, not fully.
  \item \textbf{\#8 US vs.\ UTC dates:} unresolved; with no times of day, a one-day misdating is possible at block edges.
  \item \textbf{\#12 Price quirks:} impossible returns flagged by \var{bad\_px}; drop or winsorize in Stata.
\end{itemize}
